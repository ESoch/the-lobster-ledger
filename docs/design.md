# The Lobster Ledger

A private ledger for friendly wagers, with equal approval rights for the two participants. Cloudflare Workers and D1 provide shared persistence; Google OpenID Connect identifies an invited member. React and TypeScript serve a mobile-friendly interface. Leaflet provides a map with OpenStreetMap attribution. The explicit demo uses fictional records and local browser persistence, and never claims to authenticate a person.

## Ledger rules

Every bet has a UUID, a human-readable reference, exact terms, two opposing positions, an integer stake in lobster rolls, a decision date, an agreed decision method, and an optional location. The decision method is mutual agreement, a named judge, or a manually recorded ChatGPT recommendation. A named judge must be an existing group member who is not either bettor.

The ledger follows these agreement, result, and settlement rules:

1. **Agreement:** creating the bet approves its exact terms, including the selected decision method and any named judge. The other bettor must also approve before the bet becomes active.
2. **Result:** both bettors must approve the outcome before any rolls are owed. For mutual agreement, proposing the result counts as that bettor's approval. A judge's recommendation or a recorded ChatGPT recommendation starts with no bettor approvals and requires two separate confirmations. A mutually confirmed tie voids the bet without a debt.
3. **Settlement:** physical delivery requires both bettors to confirm that the original loser bought the remaining actual lobster rolls for the original winner. Alternatively, either bettor can explicitly offset confirmed reciprocal debts as described below. An offset never records a physical delivery, and it is permanent: the card and the review dialog both say it cannot be undone. Cash cannot settle a bet.

Rolls are nontransferable. Either bettor can explicitly offset equal confirmed debts between the same two people without a second approval. If Alex owes Blake two rolls and Blake owes Alex one, either can offset one in each direction, leaving Alex owing Blake one. The preview allocates oldest debts first and excludes bets awaiting delivery confirmation. Each affected bet retains its stake and result, records the shared offset ID, actor, time, linked bets and amount, and closes only when nothing remains. No physical delivery is inferred. Debts cannot be assigned to another person or settled by a third person's obligation. All-time net statistics describe wins and losses (wins minus losses); they do not change outstanding debts. The Balances page therefore calls its own figure the “Outstanding balance” (rolls owed to you minus rolls you owe) and leaves “All-time net” to the leaderboard and members. Settled results remain in all-time statistics.

Disputes return a proposal to its preceding stage without erasing its history. Only the two bettors may approve terms, approve results, dispute proposals, or confirm delivery. An appointed judge may record a recommendation only for that bet; neither another member nor a bettor may impersonate that judge or bypass the agreed decision method.

Every exit is taken by a named member, carries a reason, and stays on the record. Whoever proposed a bet (or made the latest revision of a reported past bet) can withdraw it until the other person answers; the other bettor can decline it, including a reported past bet. The person who made an open amendment, call-off, result, delivery report or readiness claim can withdraw it before anyone else approves it; the bet returns to the stage it was in. Readiness evidence stays with the bettor who added it: the other bettor cannot replace it, but can disagree with it, with a reason, which takes it off the bet until the agreed timing arrives or someone adds new evidence; the next step then tells both bettors who disagreed and why. A named judge can step down until a result is recorded; the bettors then re-appoint a judge or change the decision method by amendment, which both must confirm. A proposal that changes nothing (an identical revision, amendment or prompt) is refused rather than recorded. Spacing nobody can see (a trailing space, a doubled space) does not make a prompt new, and a legacy bet's decision date reads as 12:00 AM UTC that day, so restating it as timing is no change. Withdrawn proposals keep the declined status and are labelled by their closing event.

Text limits are shared by the forms, the rules engine and the API, and apply to trimmed text, where zero-width characters (which show nothing) count for nothing toward a minimum: titles 5–160 characters, decision criteria 10–3000, positions up to 240, reasons and evidence up to 2000, prompts and recorded ChatGPT responses up to 8000, restaurant and roll names up to 160, a restaurant's address up to 240, and a price note, a correction reason and a withdrawal reason up to 500. The two positions must differ, ignoring case, spacing and zero-width characters. A pinned location's name, or an optional field such as the expected evidence source, that holds only zero-width characters counts as empty in the forms as well: the name is asked for ("Name the location you are pinning.") and the optional field is left unset, rather than both passing the form and failing at the ledger. Timezones are stored in their canonical spelling. Confirming an amendment with a new decision date drops personal check-ins scheduled after it, and the record says so. A delivery report settles every roll still owed; the form and the delivery block state that count.

## ChatGPT prompt agreement and provenance

For a ChatGPT bet, one bettor proposes the exact prompt and the other approves it before a recommendation can be recorded. Each revision has its own prompt version, text, proposer, approvals, and server timestamp. Editing creates a new version and resets approval to the new proposer. A stale approval cannot approve revised text. Previous prompts and approvals remain in the audit history. The drafted prompt writes the verification time and wager cutoff in the agreed timezone rather than as raw UTC stamps.

A prompt awaiting approval is shown in full on the sheet, with each bettor's approval written out ("Name — Approved" and the time, or "Waiting"); an agreed prompt sits in a named, focusable scroller. Whenever the prompt does not contain the readiness evidence on the bet, whichever was recorded first, the sheet says ChatGPT will not see it, warns the bettor asked to approve such a prompt, and offers "Add evidence to the prompt", which opens a revision with the evidence filled in; the next step leads with that button (recording a response against the agreed version stays a secondary choice), the "Your move" link and the inbox name it rather than recording a response, and any revision of the prompt starts with the evidence already in it. A prompt that still quotes evidence that came off the bet (the other bettor disagreed with it, its author took it back or replaced it) is flagged in the same way ("Prompt v1 still quotes evidence you disagreed with. Revise it to take it out, which needs both approvals again."), the next step, the "Your move" link and the inbox name "Remove evidence from the prompt", and a revision starts with the removed evidence taken out, its place held by the evidence now on the bet or, when there is none, by the draft's placeholder, so new evidence never sits beside the disputed text. Only the prompt's evidence section counts: the same words as the address the bet expects its evidence at, or as a piece of a longer version of the evidence now on the bet, are not flagged or taken out. On a bet that is still waiting for agreement, a prompt that was proposed already is not approved yet: the record says each bettor approves the exact wording after agreeing to the bet, so the opponent's "Waiting" has an explanation. Revising an agreed prompt says that it replaces the agreed version and needs both approvals again, and a revised prompt notes that approvals of earlier versions do not carry over and offers "What changed since v1", the added and removed lines against the previous version as the paper trail recorded it. If the browser refuses to copy the agreed prompt, the sheet opens it, selects it and moves focus there so it can be copied by hand.

After both bettors approve the same prompt version, a participant can use it in ChatGPT and manually enter the recommendation and reasoning. The response form counts characters against the 8000-character limit rather than silently cutting a paste, and its no-winner option reads "No winner / insufficient evidence", matching what the prompt allows ChatGPT to answer. The note under a recorded response says who pasted it and whether a share link was given. The application does not call ChatGPT or certify that pasted text came from an AI. An optional source link must be an official HTTPS ChatGPT share URL; the server records it without fetching it. The recommendation identifies its authenticated recorder, timestamp, and approved prompt version. Prompt consent is separate from result consent: both bettors must still confirm the recommendation before a debt exists.

All commands are authorized on the server. Each event records server time and actor. Optimistic concurrency and atomic writes prevent double approvals and overwritten history. Audit rows cannot be edited or deleted by application queries. This is an auditable social ledger; it does not certify legal enforceability.

## Experience

Overview, searchable ledger, direct balances, leaderboard, members, and a map of wager locations. An action inbox is visible on the overview: it lists approvals and decisions waiting on the member, labelled with the sheet's own button words when one button is the next step, or with a neutral "Answer / Review / Confirm a …" label when the sheet offers an approve-or-decline pair. Owed rolls are not inbox duties: they stay under the ledger's To settle filter and on the bet, where the debtor records the delivery or an offset. While a bet waits on its timing, the sheet puts the evidence and check-in tools in the next step, with Add readiness evidence as the primary action, so a passed claim cutoff or check-in never hides behind a disclosure. Every exit the rules allow is also in the next step: the proposer can withdraw an unanswered bet, the other bettor can decline it (including a reported past bet), the named judge can step down until a result exists, the proposer of an open amendment, call-off, result or delivery report, or of readiness evidence, can take it back, the other bettor can disagree with that evidence ("Disagree with Sarah’s evidence", above a form that quotes it) instead of adding their own over it, and "Call off this bet" stays visible under the next step of any bet in play. Each exit keeps one name from its button through the form title to the submit. Exit forms ask for a reason of at least ten characters, say what will happen and that it stays on the ledger, and put a danger-styled button after Cancel. When a judge steps down, the bettors are told why and offered an amendment that names a new judge or method, and the method card stops presenting anyone as the judge: the judge reads "You stepped down as judge", everyone else "Judge stepped down" with the name and "No judge is set"; once the bet is called off or declined it only says the judge stepped down before a recommendation was recorded, since nobody can name a new one. A declined, withdrawn or disputed record shows who did it, why and when, next to the proposal a dispute cleared; the header badge reads Withdrawn or Delivery disputed where that applies, and the stepper ends on Declined, Withdrawn, "Called off · nothing owed" or, for a no-winner result both bettors confirmed, "No winner · nothing owed"; each step also states in words whether it is done, current, not yet reached or the step that closed the bet. A called-off record says who proposed it, who agreed and when. One-click approvals are bound to the version the member read: if someone else changes the bet while the sheet is open (a personal check-in aside), the approval waits until they acknowledge the newer version, focus then moves to the approval, and a newly proposed prompt opens for reading. The waiting approval is held with `aria-disabled` rather than disabled, described by the note, so a change that arrives while it has focus never drops focus to the page: focus moves on to the acknowledgement ("I’ve read prompt v2"), which sits just before it. A draft overtaken by someone else's change keeps its text but never its ticked confirmations, which were given against the older record: Use the latest record moves it onto the new version and focuses its first field, and when the action no longer applies the text can move into the one that does (calling the bet off, or disagreeing with a result filed meanwhile that names a different winner). A result or delivery draft overtaken by the other bettor's matching report points to that report to confirm instead of a dispute, and readiness evidence overtaken by the other bettor's points to theirs, to disagree with only if it does not show the answer. When a change is still refused because the record moved on, the sheet shows a note that names the latest event and says the change was not recorded (or that the same member already made it elsewhere), moves focus to it, and drops the generic red error. How it works and the Balances page read the house rules from one shared text, so the offset rule is stated the same way in both places; How it works also explains declining, withdrawing, amending and calling off a bet. It never prompts a bettor while a judged bet is waiting on the judge, and it brings an event or evidence bet up for review once its claim cutoff has passed. Selecting a bet opens its terms, participants, selected decision method, prompt agreement or judge's recommendation when applicable, next action, and complete event timeline. When the next move is the reader's, a "Your move" link at the top of the sheet names it and jumps to the next step. A compact new-bet form collects all required terms before creating a proposal. The interface keeps prompt consent, result confirmation, and delivery confirmation distinct. Export includes the ledger, prompt versions, adjudication provenance, and timestamps.

The sheet's records say who did what and when, in one vocabulary: a judge records a _recommendation_, in the sheet, the inbox, toasts, the stored record and the server's refusals. A member outside the bet has no next step, so a note says whose move it is ("Waiting for Sarah to agree.") before who can act, and names the judge's part only while the recommendation is still to come, and not a second time when the line before it has just said that the judge records it. The facts row names how a bet was entered only when that was recorded. The delivery and disagreement forms speak to the reader ("3 rolls Sarah still owes you") and say that the rolls stay owed until one of you records a delivery and the other confirms it. Waiting lines name the bettor who still has to act, and that bettor reads that their answer is needed rather than a line about waiting for themselves. The decision timing record answers whether the bet can be reviewed yet (Ready for review, Check-in due, Cutoff passed, Not yet or Paused), marks a passed cutoff (to a member outside the bet it says only that the cutoff has passed, not to go and add evidence), and shows a personal check-in only to that bettor while the bet is in play. A result, a delivery report and readiness evidence are all _disagreed with_: the button, the form, the toast, the header badge ("Delivery disagreement") and the closure block use that one verb. The closure block under a declined, withdrawn or disagreed-with bet speaks to the reader ("You disagreed with Casey's recommendation", "Blair disagreed with your recommendation" to the judge, "You declined this proposal") and quotes what was cleared for what it said ("Earlier recommendation · Casey: Avery wins: the reason") without the instruction that both bettors must confirm a result that no longer exists. Proposed changes are shown side by side with the current agreement, each part marked Changed, Unchanged, Added or Removed. Result and delivery blocks name the proposer or reporter, show each bettor's signature and time, state the rolls involved, and stay visibly neutral until both bettors confirm. The judge's card states the recommendation itself, and says that a no-winner recommendation leaves no rolls owed; the judge reads "You are the judge" on the method card, and a ChatGPT bet's method card says the prompt is a separate step once the bet is agreed. A bet that was declined or withdrawn has no method card, since no result will be decided on it (unless its judge stepped down first, which is part of what happened). Before a bet is agreed, the timing record says that its timing, cutoff and check-in are part of what each bettor agrees to. The position cards mark who has agreed to the terms only while the terms are being agreed, since afterwards both marks would always be lit. The location link says it opens OpenStreetMap in a new tab, and a pin the bettors dropped shows its coordinates instead of a placeholder address. Section headings in the records share one size, and on a phone the next step's buttons share one width. A requested correction on a reported past bet is an amber notice rather than an error: it names who asked and when, quotes the request and says that neither bettor can confirm until the history is revised (to anyone who is not a bettor on it, that the version cannot be confirmed until then), and the bettor who asked reads "You asked for a correction" there and in the next step, where Revise history is the primary button for either bettor; the proposer is never offered a correction of their own report. A reported past bet that was declined or withdrawn reads "History declined" or "History withdrawn" rather than awaiting confirmation: the bettor who closed it shows Declined or Withdrew with the time, a bettor who never answered shows Did not answer, and a correction that had been asked stays on the record without saying that it blocks anything. A revised history says which revision it is, who reported it, which correction it answers and what it changed from the revision before ("Stake: 1 → 2"; free text is marked edited). In the paper trail each replaced revision lists the same changes and opens as "Revision 1 (replaced here)", with who reported and confirmed it, any correction asked of it, each bettor's position by name, a decision date it only named as a day, and the exact pin of its location. The Revise history form starts with the correction it answers in the requester's words, says the earlier version stays in the paper trail, offers the saved places the way New bet does (a newly pinned place gets its own identity, so the saved place it resembles is untouched), and keeps a decision date the record only named as a day unless its time is changed. Its title and positions wrap rather than scroll sideways (Enter does not add a line to them), and the review timing, like the outcome and delivery of a past bet, is set aside rather than discarded when the stage changes. When a reported past bet is waiting on the other bettor, the next step explains its choices once, under the buttons: confirm if the history is right, request a correction to ask the proposer for a change (nothing can be confirmed until it is revised), or revise it yourself and both confirm the new version; the proposer is told that revising replaces their report and the earlier one stays in the paper trail. The correction form says the same before it is sent. The confirm form opens with one line on what the history comes to ("Ellis won. Alex owes Ellis 1 lobster roll.") above the boxes it asks about, and says "You are the second confirmation" with what confirming does; in Add existing bet and Revise history, sending is the first confirmation and the other bettor is named as the one who confirms next.

The paper trail never changes stored text; it formats it for reading. A disagreement with a result, a delivery report or readiness evidence reads "Disagreed with the …" (the stored record still says "Disputed …"). Member ids become names (including the other bettor of an offset, whose internal offset id is left out), check-ins appear in their own timezone rather than as UTC stamps, a follow-up sentence starts its own line, https links are clickable, and a proposed prompt or a long reasoning sits behind a disclosure after the attribution line. New audit text names only what a step included, for example the stages of a reported past bet or what an approved amendment cleared. The concurrency counter is shown as the record revision.

The places map keeps pins at least 44px apart and groups any that would still overlap. A group pin carries a small pin glyph beside its number, because it counts places while a single pin counts bets. It keeps its view and popups across ledger refreshes: a change at another place leaves an open popup alone, and a change at its own place (a new count, a new name) is written into it where it is, so focus inside it stays and the map never pans back to it. It selects a pin from the keyboard just as it does from a click. On touch screens one finger scrolls the page and two fingers move the map; the overview shows a static preview. Place counts include only agreed bets, with proposals awaiting agreement listed separately. When pinning a new location, saved places appear for reference only. Use map center works once the map is zoomed in, and a click on a map still zoomed far out zooms toward that spot instead of pinning it, so a pin is never a guess hundreds of kilometres wide; the hidden status says "Zoomed in on that area. Select the exact spot." and keeps saying it through the zoom, until the member moves the view or the pin is placed. The picker links OpenStreetMap once, in the map's attribution. The form confirms the pin by name; the line under the map follows the name as it is typed, and a screen reader hears it when the name field is left. Choosing a place on the Places page lists how many wagers it keeps above the table, with a Show all locations control. The table lists every proposal at the place, so the heading names the ones the place's count leaves out ("Showing 5 of 16 wagers · 1 withdrawn", "· 2 declined"). The page order is the drawn order at every width: below 1001px the map sits above the place list and comes first in the page; wider screens draw the list on the left with the map to its right and put the list first, so Tab goes list, map, table. A screen that turns across 1000px (a tablet rotated) swaps them without reloading the map, and focus stays where it was. The list scrolls inside its panel at every width (below 1001px in a box no taller than 420px or 60% of the screen), so many places never push the wagers table far down. Below 1001px a tap on a place scrolls up to the map. Enter or Space shows the map from its top edge, clear of the pinned top bar, and keeps the chosen row on screen by scrolling only the place list; when the two cannot both be on screen (a short phone, or a row near the end of a long list), focus moves to the chosen pin, beside its popup. The full map's OpenStreetMap link and its attribution sit in a strip under the map rather than over it, so a pin that pans to the bottom edge is never half beneath them (the overview preview and the picker keep Leaflet's corner attribution); Tab visits the link, then the attribution, the way the strip is drawn. A popup that has to pan the map to fit rests the selected pin well above the bottom edge. A map whose tiles fail to load says so and offers Retry, which leaves focus on the map.

Private live mode and the switchable demo are clearly distinguished. Existing members can create single-use invitations that expire after 72 hours, share links using the device share sheet or SMS, and onboard another person with Google. The invitation form asks for the name the new member will appear under: one rule for the form, the demo and the API trims it, collapses inner whitespace and accepts 1 to 100 characters (a blank name is met with "Enter a name.", and the length is only mentioned, as "Use 100 characters or fewer.", to someone who went over it), and the form turns away a name another member already goes by (compared without regard to case) with a message under the field; the API refuses the same name with a 400, since a form only knows the members it has loaded, and shows that refusal under the field too, announced as it appears. It also refuses a name another open invitation is already waiting for, so two links never admit two people under one name. The member is admitted under that name, with their Google profile name as the fallback (for the owner, for an invitation with no usable name, and for a name someone took after it was typed). Whichever name is used, a name another member already goes by is numbered ("Alex (2)"), so the fallback can never put two people on one row. When the invitation is created the dialog's description says it is ready and expires in 3 days, and focus moves to the "invitation is ready" message, since the button that was pressed is gone with the form. Automated SMS is a separate provider integration requiring consent and configuration; no messages are sent during development.

The Members page: one card per member, with a summary line built from the ledger (bets in play, and from the reader's seat who owes whom: "2 bets in play · owes you 3 rolls"; opposing debts stay separate, as on the Balances page, so a pair that owe each other reads "owes you 3 rolls · you owe 1 roll" rather than a netted figure), a "Bet with <name>" button that opens the new-bet dialog with that person already chosen (not shown on the reader's own card), and a "View bets" button that opens the ledger narrowed to that name. The reader's own heading reads "Name (you)". The three figures sit on the foot of every card, so cards of different heights keep them level; a win rate with no decided bet is a dash rather than 0%, a net of zero is neutral rather than green, and one footnote says what "All-time net" counts. The action that opens the invitation form is called "Invite a member" everywhere it appears (the sidebar, the intro, the dashed card and the dialog's title), and the dashed card is a button holding only phrasing content. In a live ledger a "Pending invitations" panel under the cards lists the invitations the reader made that can still be used, each with its expiry and a Revoke button; revoking ends the link at once and moves focus to the confirmation; revoking one that was already used or over (in another window, say) drops the row, reloads the list and says it is no longer open. Only the hash of a link is stored, so a link cannot be shown a second time: to replace a lost one, revoke it and invite again. The demo has no invitations to list.

## Visual direction

Deep harbor green #153b3e, lobster red #bd4938, oyster #f7f8f5, white #ffffff, graphite #27393b, and muted sage #7e9188. Manrope for the interface and Fraunces for the masthead and main greeting. A quiet sidebar anchors a generous workspace; the balance summary is the focal point. Use a hand-drawn roll motif sparingly, hairline separators for ledger rows, calm status badges, and restrained surface depth. Typography and data hierarchy carry the design. Minimum 44px principal touch targets, keyboard focus, reduced motion, and mobile navigation.

The command-line and AI-connection guides follow the same anatomy as every other page: a `.page-heading` with the serif title, then numbered steps and aside cards built as titled `.panel`s on the app's type scale, with body text, notes and errors keeping their own inks in both themes. The intro under the title runs the width of the column on a phone (the app's 240px cap is for headings that share a row with buttons), the aside sits beside the steps only from 1101px, where each column has room for its text and its commands, and a vendor guide that stands alone in its paragraph is a 44px target, as the welcome page's two guide links are. Every copyable command uses one copy block with a reserved status line, one "Copied" message and an error tone, and wraps only between words, so a flag such as `--dry-run` or a host name never splits. An invalid or expired CLI sign-in request shows as a problem, with a warning icon, the error red and a copyable `lobster auth login`; so does one the server refuses as invalid when Allow is pressed, or whose answer does not lead back to the CLI, since pressing Allow again cannot change either. A lost connection or busy server keeps the card and Allow (which stays focusable while it works, so focus is not dropped), and an ended session swaps Allow for sign-in. When a card changes this way, focus moves to its heading; when Cancel or Dismiss closes it, focus moves to the page title instead of falling to the page body. Pages the Worker renders itself (sign-in errors, MCP consent, Connected apps and browser visits to `/oauth/authorize` or `/mcp`) share one template with the app's palette, dark by default with a light override for a light device preference, and the lobster mark. Each sign-in failure has its own heading and next step: a Google account that is not a member is told to use a different one, a failed or cancelled sign-in can be started again, and an unusable invitation, a throttled network or an outage point only back to the ledger.

The CLI sign-in request card sits under the page heading so the h1 stays first, takes focus when a request arrives or fails, and shows how long the request lasts. It says that Cancel only closes the card, since the waiting terminal stops with Ctrl+C. The demo version explains why it has no Allow button and links to the live ledger, in place of the guide’s own demo notice so the page says it once; the demo never reads, forces open or clears a real request waiting in the same tab. In the connected-devices list a revoke moves focus into its confirmation and back, and a failed revoke is reported on its own row with a retry for that device. A sign-in button that cannot work is disabled with the true reason beside it: Google sign-in isn’t set up on an instance without it, and sign-in can’t be reached when the ledger’s configuration fails to load. Links that open another site in a new tab say so.

Keyboard and screen-reader behavior: every dialog and bet panel returns focus to the control that opened it, including when the browser did not focus that control on click. A dialog opened from the phone menu returns to the Open navigation button, a dialog opened from another dialog falls back to that dialog's opener, and the page heading takes focus when no opener is left or when the dialog sent the reader to another page. Forms share one anatomy, in dialogs and in the bet sheet:

- One footer, `.modal-footer`: Cancel, then the primary button. It stays pinned to the bottom of a dialog or the sheet while a long form scrolls, and it holds the form's own error (a refusal from the ledger), so the error is in view wherever the form is scrolled. On a phone the two buttons share the row and the primary wraps onto its own row when both labels do not fit. Nothing pinned hides the field in use: as focus moves, the dialog measures its pinned footer (taller when it holds an error or the discard question), the bet sheet's pinned heading, a dialog's pinned discard question and any toast lying over it, and scrolls a field reached with Tab, or the field a refused submit moves to, into the part that stays clear, whole when it fits (the browser alone would show only a text box's caret line, and Tab wrapping round to Close would not scroll at all). A control in a pinned part (the sheet's Close, the discard question's buttons, a footer button) is left where it is pinned, so Shift+Tab to Close does not move the open form out of sight, and a footer button that Tab wraps round to after its form has scrolled away is brought back into view. A form opened in the sheet comes into view whole, with the line that says what submitting does just above its buttons.
- Forms check their fields when submitted instead of relying on browser bubbles or a disabled submit. Each problem is named under its field in friendly words that use the shared text limits on trimmed text ("Use at least 5 characters."), the field is marked `aria-invalid` and described by the message, focus moves to the first problem, and a message clears as soon as its field changes. A timezone ("Mars/Base") and a ChatGPT share link are also checked when the field is left, and the share-link message shows a good example. EST and MST are turned away with a pointer to a place name, since they stay on one offset all year while most people who type them change clocks; short names that do follow daylight saving (CET, PST8PDT) and HST, which Hawaii keeps all year, are accepted. A check-in must be a time still ahead (an amendment may keep the check-in it already has). A date and time field takes years 2000 to 2100 only, and a date outside them is named in words ("Choose a date between 2000 and 2100."). The Roll prices form is checked the same way, with its price named in dollars and cents. A reported past bet is checked with the ledger's own history rules before it is sent, so a date in the future, an outcome or delivery dated before an earlier stage, an unreadable month or an evidence link that is not HTTPS is named under that date or field in words that say which date it is ("The outcome can’t be decided before the bet was made."). Each date asks how well it is remembered first, then takes an exact day, a month or a year that stops at today; switching between them keeps what was typed (a day becomes its month, and comes back if it still fits), and where a browser shows the month as a plain text box it reads 2024-03, March 2024 or 3/2024. A date also starts no earlier than 1900 and no earlier than the stage before it (the outcome and the delivery cannot be dated before the bet, or the delivery before the outcome), so the date picker greys those days out and a typed one is named in the same words as above. Add existing bet opens on "Where does this bet stand?", which says what each answer records and who confirms it; changing the stage keeps the outcome, delivery, review timing and earlier-decision text already typed (set aside while they do not apply, and neither sent nor checked) and the terms tick, and asks again only for the confirmation worded for a stage. Its examples and the criteria prompt are written for a bet that already happened. Fields that have no slot of their own for a message (the confirmation boxes) are listed by the buttons and linked the same way. A new location that is named but not yet pinned says so under the name field, above the map (in New bet and in Revise history), and choosing another location clears that message. The Revise history form names a missing title, stake, position or set of criteria under that field in the same words New bet uses, and a date's message sits the same small gap below the date that a field's does. Bringing in a bet that looks like one already on the ledger (the same two people and title, at the same stake, or at any stake when the two were made within a week of each other; titles are compared ignoring case, punctuation, accents and a leading “the”, “a” or “an”) shows the match and asks for a tick that it is a different bet before it can be sent; the other bettor sees the same match and tick when confirming it. This check is made in the app: the rules engine, the CLI and MCP do not refuse a duplicate.
- A submit is never disabled for missing input. While a change is recorded it says "Recording…" with `aria-busy` and keeps focus, the fields and Cancel are held, a second press does nothing, and the sheet stays open on Close, Escape or a click outside until the change is recorded; a polite status line in the sheet says the change is being recorded and that the sheet can be closed once it is saved. A plain disabled button shows a not-allowed cursor, never a wait cursor. A draft overtaken by a newer record keeps its submit focusable and described by the note that explains what changed.
- Hints sit under their control in one regular-weight 12px style and are linked with `aria-describedby`, never inside the label, so they do not become part of a field's name; avatars beside the position labels stay out of the names too. "Optional" sits beside its label text. Required fields keep the `required` attribute. Field borders are 3:1 against the field and the surface in both themes, and an invalid field takes the error ink. A unit or currency ("$", "lobster roll" or "lobster rolls") sits inside the field's box. Every one-line control (text, date, date and time, select) is 46px tall, so a date beside its timezone or precision lines up.
- Log a visit (Roll prices) opens fresh each time, on the restaurant being viewed, with focus on the price and that restaurant's last report under it as a hint ("Last reported $28.00 on Sep 11, 2026 by Alex."), never as a prefilled value. Choose a restaurant and Add a restaurant are a labelled group of pressed-state buttons; a ledger with no restaurants opens on adding one, with focus on its name, and typing a name already on the record (ignoring case and spacing) offers "Use Harbor Shack" before a second copy is made, which chooses it and moves focus to the price. A new restaurant is saved in the same write as its first price, so a refused price never leaves an empty restaurant. The price is typed text, "Menu price for one roll" with "Before tax and tip." under it, so scrolling over it never changes it, and the item field suggests the names already used at that restaurant. The empty price field shows an example ("e.g. 28.00") that cannot be mistaken for a typed value. The date asks how exact it is first (an exact day, a month and year, or a year), starts on today by the member's own clock and refuses a date still to come or before 1900. A restaurant or roll named with only spaces, or a pasted name past its limit, is refused on the website (under its own field), in the demo and over MCP, and nothing is saved. Reporting the same visit again at the same price keeps the report already there; at another price the dialog asks, just above its buttons, whether to correct the earlier report or save a separate purchase, and the question stays until it is answered. A correction says which report it replaces and will not save an unchanged copy; it takes an optional reason, shown on the correction's row; the replaced report stays on record under the restaurant's Earlier reports, its successor is tagged Corrected and takes focus once the correction is saved, and visit dates always carry their year ("Aug 2026", "2025"). A price outside the $8 to $90 a roll usually costs, or about ten times or a tenth of the last live report of the same roll at that restaurant, is asked about just above the buttons before it is saved ("You entered $2.50. Did you mean $25.00?"), with the likely price as a one-tap fix and "Save $2.50 anyway" to keep it (when the likely price is the one the report being corrected already has, the one-tap answer is "Keep my $25.00 report", which closes the dialog without a change); this question and the one about a visit already reported are notices in the gold tint, not errors, and are still announced; answering one question with another following (Correct my report leading to a price question, Save anyway leading to the visit question) hands keyboard focus to the first button of the new one, so Tab carries on from there instead of from the top of the dialog, while a question that first appears leaves focus where the member was; the website and MCP ask the same question, and nothing is ever hidden from the history for looking unusual. A member's own live report also offers Withdraw: a dialog asks why (1 to 500 characters), says the report stays on record under Earlier reports, and confirms with the danger button. A withdrawn report leaves the latest price, the change, the list row, the chart and every count, and appears under Earlier reports with its date and reason; focus goes back to Withdraw on Cancel and to Earlier reports once the report has moved there. A report entered through an AI connection carries a quiet "via AI connection" note.
- New bet asks, in order: what the bet is; who is on the other side and the rolls at stake; the two positions ("Your position", "Sarah's position"); what counts as proof; who makes the call (with a note when choosing an opponent removes them as judge); when review opens ("What opens review?" with the matching sentence right under it; a date and its timezone sit side by side when there is room, and switching between a date, an event and evidence keeps what was typed in each; an agreement that only ever named a decision date is amended with that day filled in as 12:00 AM UTC, and the form says so under the time); then the category and where the bet was made. Once a bet is proposed its sheet opens at the next step ("Waiting for Sarah to agree"), scrolled into view with focus on that heading, rather than at the top of the record.
- Opening an inline form in the sheet focuses its first field. Cancel, Escape and a recorded submit clear the whole form (text, choices and errors) and return focus to the button that opened it, or to the next step when that button is gone; a change recorded from the collapsed tools (such as readiness evidence) moves on to the next step, while cancelling returns to the tools' summary; after a one-click approval focus moves to the next step too. Choosing a new judge or method after a judge steps down opens the amendment form with who decides first, and focus starts there. The first Escape leaves an open form and the next one closes the sheet; when something has been typed, that first Escape, or the form's Cancel, asks "Discard what you have entered?" (Keep editing or Discard) in a gold caution, not an error's red, and Discard closes only the form. Close or a click outside the sheet asks the same, and Discard there closes the sheet. So do Back (the swipe-back gesture on a phone) and a typed #hash or link: the sheet stays open on its own page, keeps its history entry and asks, and Discard then goes on to where Back or the address was going. A sheet that is recording a change holds Back as it holds Close. While any dialog holds unsaved input, reloading or closing the tab brings up the browser's own question. In the sheet the question is a row of the pinned heading, under the title and Close, so it stays in view without covering Close. The New bet and Add existing bet dialogs ask on Escape, Close, a click outside and their own Cancel. Keep editing returns focus to the field that had it last, even when a click outside has already taken focus off it, and the dialog box itself wears no focus ring. A sheet is announced as "Bet details" with a description that names this bet (reference, title, status and stake). The prompt editor counts characters against the 8,000 limit and, like a pasted ChatGPT response, says how far over it is rather than cutting the text off; every other note in the sheet (delivery details, evidence, the reason for declining, disagreeing or proposing changes) does the same against 2,000, and the submit waits until it is trimmed, and a failed copy of the agreed prompt is shown by the copy button and cleared once a copy works.

Notifications: every confirmation and every error appears in one toast region rendered outside the app root. An open dialog or bet sheet hides the rest of the page from assistive technology and ignores the pointer outside itself, so a toast inside the page could be neither announced nor dismissed. Confirmations use a polite status region; errors, such as a bet link that cannot be found or a failed sign-out, use an assertive alert region with a warning icon and a red border. Each message says what happened: the people involved, the winner and loser, the rolls, the amount and restaurant, or the file and how many bets it holds. "Recorded on the ledger." is only a fallback for an action type the app has no wording for, and the build fails when a bet action has none. A toast stays at least six seconds (about 60ms a character for longer lines), pauses while the pointer or focus is on it, and starts over when the same message is shown again. An open dialog or bet sheet keeps Tab inside itself, so there Escape dismisses the toast before it closes the dialog, and moving through the dialog with Tab holds a toast that lies clear of the dialog, as focusing it would (one lying over the dialog keeps its time, so it never hides the dialog for long, and nor does one beside a dialog that has shrunk under 240px tall in a very short window, so the dialog gets its room back); the pointer, or focus coming back out of the dialog, lets it go. It sits beside the sidebar and, with a bet sheet open, to the left of the sheet; where there is no room for that, it sits at the foot of the sheet, which keeps space under its last entry: as much as the toast is tall (it takes more lines in a narrow window), so the sheet can always be scrolled until nothing is under the toast, and the space eases away when the toast goes. While any other dialog is open the toast never lies over its title, pinned buttons or Close button: on a window 1154px or wider it sits at the top left, beside the dialog, narrowing to fit; on a narrower one (a tablet or a phone) it sits at the foot of the window, below the dialog, which moves up and shrinks by the toast's height (its form scrolls inside) and takes the room back, easing, when the toast goes. On a phone it otherwise sits above the safe area.

Overview, Ledger and Inbox panels:

- **Next moves are one component.** Over to you (Overview) and Your next moves (Activity inbox) render the same action item: the bet, the sheet's own verb, the rolls at stake, who is waiting ("Alex is waiting for you", or "Alex vs James · you are the judge" for a named judge) and when the bet last changed. The inbox row adds the status chip. Both list duties by type (answer a proposal, then confirm a result or delivery, then decide or gather evidence; owed rolls are not next moves, they stay under To settle) and, within a type, the longest-waiting bet first. Over to you shows three and says "Showing 3 of 15" with a link to the inbox; its footer link stays at the foot of the panel however short the list is, its cards line up with the heading, and the "N actions waiting" line is a status region, so a change in the count is announced. When nothing waits on the member, both places say the same thing. On a wide, tall window a short list of next moves stays in view while the activity feed scrolls past it. Waiting on lists agreed bets by the date it is sorted by (the decision time, the initial check-in both bettors agreed, "Your check-in" when you set one yourself, or the claim cutoff, each named as such and marked "passed" once it has gone by; a check-in is a bettor's reminder, so a judged bet reads and sorts by its decision time for the judge), says what an event or evidence bet waits for, keeps undated bets at the end, and "View all N waiting" expands the list in place, with a chevron that turns over rather than the arrow that links to another page. A judged bet whose time has come is on the judge, not the bettors: it asks nothing of them, so it is not one of their duties and is not in the "N actions waiting" count, but it stays in both bettors' Waiting on, reading "Waiting on Sarah’s recommendation since Oct 10, 1:00 PM EDT" (the decision time, in the zone it was set in, or the moment the evidence was marked ready, in the reader's own zone and named, "since Oct 12, 8:05 AM PDT", so both rows read in the same shape), instead of vanishing when it matters; for the judge it is a duty. A bet whose judge stepped down stays in Over to you, where both bettors must act. In a next move a count and its noun ("1 roll", "Updated 2 hours ago") are joined by a no-break space, so a narrow card never ends a line on a bare number.
- **Page order is focus order.** The Overview markup runs Over to you, the latest wagers, Waiting on, Bragging rights, the map. On wide screens the grid seats Over to you on the right of the first row beside the wagers and Waiting on across the row beneath, so Tab goes from Over to you to the wagers and from there only ever down the page; below 1001px the single column is the page order. No CSS `order` separates what is read from what is tabbed.
- **Selection is exposed to assistive technology.** The ledger status chips, the Overview tabs, the Places views and the place rows are toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`) inside a named group. The Places views are not a tablist, because they have no tab panels. Chip counts follow the search, "Only my bets" and "Entered via", so each count is the number of rows that chip shows, and a status line under the controls announces "2 of 14 bets". Chips wrap on narrow screens instead of scrolling out of sight (under 361px each chip trims its inner padding and gaps so the six make two rows rather than three and "Other closed" does not sit alone), and the chips, search row, result line, table and footer share one gutter so their left edges line up. On a phone (520px or narrower) the controls give back space around themselves so the first bet is in view on a short screen: less padding above the chips and below the search row, a shorter table header, and the result line folded away while nothing is filtered (the "All bets" chip already says the count; the line stays in its live region for screen readers and shows once a filter narrows the list). Every target keeps its 44px. The "Only my bets" checkbox is drawn at 24px and the search input keeps a 24px line inside its 44px field, so neither is a smaller target than WCAG 2.5.8 allows. "Only my bets" and the Overview's "Your bets" tab include a bet you were named to judge. A ledger row leads its meta line with "Your move" when the bet is waiting on the viewer, and an Added date names its year when it is not this one. The search field is a search input, so a browser may offer its own clear button.
- **Empty states say what is true.** An empty ledger reads "No bets on the ledger yet" with New bet and no filters; a search that matches nothing keeps Clear filters, which returns focus to the search field. On the Overview of an empty ledger New bet appears once, in the latest wagers. The inbox says "Nothing to do yet" (with New bet) on a ledger with no bets and "You’re all caught up" once there are bets, and an empty feed says "No activity yet". Overview stats pluralise every count, show a dash for a win rate with no decided bets, and caption zero values accordingly.
- **The activity feed is bounded.** It shows ten entries and "Show 10 more", clamps each entry to three lines, and names each button by the bet title plus the first 120 characters of the entry. An entry leads with the bet, then what happened, then when (with the year and the time, since the feed is the signed record) and who, and each row, like a next move, says that it opens a dialog. Status chips carry their own icon and tone for every label, and one label function (used by the inbox, the bet sheet and exports) says "History needs correction" when a correction was requested. The ledger and Balances tables say "Needs correction" and the compact tables "Correction": their status column is a fixed pixel track (every row is its own grid, so a content-sized one would not line up with the header), at least 146px wide wherever the table is not stacked under its title, which holds the widest label, "Confirming delivery", with its padding. A chip wider than its track spills over both edges, so `src/bet-table-css.test.ts` pins the track and a DOM test caps the label length.
- **Small details that carry meaning.** Bragging rights is an ordered list that writes each rank in words, keeps "Ellis you" from reading as one word, and signs a negative total with a true minus sign in neutral ink, since All-time net is not a balance. It ranks as the Leaderboard does: only members with a confirmed result, each of the top three places is the Leaderboard's numbered disc, so gold and bronze differ by more than a tint, tied members share a rank ("Rank 1 (tied)" for a screen reader, "tied" in sight), the list holds everyone who holds a top-three place (a tie for third shows both, up to five rows, then "+1 more tied for 3rd"; when a longer tie is cut, your own row takes the last of the five places rather than being the one left out, since the members it hides are level with it), and before any result is confirmed it says "No confirmed results yet" instead of handing out medals. The Leaderboard's column header is set like the ledger table's (same padding, a rule beneath and none above). On the Overview the figures in the stat cards stay level with the hero card's and each caption drops to the foot; at small-laptop widths the hero's roll moves above its link; a map title stays on one line and its link drops beneath it when the panel is narrow; on a phone the two footer statements stack. Short captions (the stat cards', and the empty states' titles and descriptions) are balanced with `text-wrap: balance`, so their last line is never one word ("to.", "here.") where a browser supports it. Under Offset history each bet reference sits on a 24px line, so the stacked links are not 3px apart.

Leaderboard, Roll prices and Lobster prices:

Tied members share a competition rank (1, 1, 3) and the same numbered medal, and the lead sentence, set in page ink straight under the title and above the table, gives the leader's own figure and the margin over everyone level in second, or names every leader ("Ellis leads with +2 net rolls, 4 ahead of Sarah.", "Ellis and Olivia share the lead with +1 net roll each."). Other items are named beside it ("Also reported: Connecticut lobster roll $30.00"); every report stays in the list. The change is a pill that says its direction ("Up $2.00 since Jul 18, 2026", "Down …", "No change …") and is measured only against a visit whose period does not overlap the newest one, so a month report and a day in that month are never a change. The chart is a labelled group of points: one tab stop (the latest visit), with the arrow keys, Home and End moving along them, and each point's value shows on hover, on keyboard focus and when it is tapped. The chart is drawn at its measured width, so its 12px labels stay 12px; visits are spaced by date (a month or year sits mid-period), the price scale uses round steps, a level price is captioned "No change across 3 reports", and one visit shows a note instead of a chart. The chart, that note and the change count visits the same way: reports whose periods overlap are one visit (a day reported twice is its later report, and a month or year gives way to any finer report inside it), so a July report and one from July 20 are a single point, not a line. Restaurant rows say the latest price, item, date and report count ("$28.00 · Classic lobster roll", "Sep 11, 2026 · 5 reports"), are muted and tagged Old report when that visit ended more than twelve months ago, expose the selection with `aria-pressed`, and gain a Lowest price first toggle from four restaurants. Prices sit in one column with Correct in a fixed slot after them, held at every restaurant for a member who has a live report anywhere (so the column does not move when the restaurant changes); a long unbroken note or item name wraps inside its row; at 520px and below Correct moves under its price, so the item and its date keep the row's width. Below 1000px the price panel stacks under the list and scrolls into view when a restaurant is chosen or a report lands on one. A restaurant with no reports offers "Add a price for …", an empty price book offers Log a visit, and Log a visit is a secondary button beside New bet. `#places/prices` opens the tab, and switching tabs keeps the address in step without adding history.
- **Lobster prices reads at every width and by touch.** The stat figures never break from their sign (one minus sign, "−", in the price and the percentage); between 761 and 1000px the cards sit two across with the third full width. A late annual update is a gold warning notice with an icon, set apart from the cards; the source panel keeps the last successful check, and promises a weekly check only when the automatic check made the last one (a source file checked by hand says so, and nothing about weekly). On the chart a mouse inspects on press and hover, a tap or a sideways drag inspects on touch, and a vertical swipe still scrolls the page (`touch-action: pan-y`); the inspected year is a round HTML marker, and year and price labels sit at their data positions on round steps. A preliminary year is drawn apart from the final ones: the line turns dashed at the last final year, the point is open (and so is the inspected marker while it sits on one), and the caption says so. The year slider is named by its visible label "Inspect year" and speaks the year and price (`aria-valuetext`), so the readout above the chart is not a live region and a slider step is heard once; a year inspected with a mouse or a tap, which has no value text of its own, is said in a hidden status, which a later slider step clears so it never keeps reading an older year. The annual table is a named, focusable scroll region, no taller than 480px, with right-aligned figures, a sticky year and a heading row that stays in view while the 76 years scroll; on phones the Status column ("Preliminary" or "Final") folds in under the year, and at 380px and below the cells tighten and a heading may take two lines, so the four columns left fit a 320px screen. While columns wait off-screen (larger text, say) the right edge carries a shade, drawn light in the dark theme. The two source links say in words that they open a new tab, and on phones the three links stack as ruled rows instead of wrapping. Restaurant roll prices opens Places on its Roll prices tab through the app's navigation, and Export CSV confirms with a toast.

Signed-out and failure states: the page says what is actually wrong and offers the next step. Signing in is unavailable only when the server says Google sign-in is not connected; a config request that fails, times out (read requests give up after 15 seconds) or returns a 5xx is a connection problem, so Google stays enabled and one notice says "Can’t reach the ledger" with a Try again button that runs the whole load again. A 401 from the session request is the ordinary signed-out visit and prints nothing. A member whose session is valid but whose ledger does not load never meets the sign-in page: they see "Can’t reach the ledger" and Try again, with the raw status line left out. The public guides show no notice while the sign-in check runs, so nothing flashes and the page does not jump; the not-connected or can’t-reach notice appears once the answer is known (neither guide repeats that sign-in is not connected in its own words, so each carries that one notice, not two). A visitor who signed in on this browser before and now gets a 401 is told "Your session ended" with the way back in, kept apart from the failure notices (a browser-side note, set at sign-in and cleared by an intentional sign-out, is the only way to tell them from a first-time visitor; it holds no personal data). A link to a page of the ledger followed while signed out makes the welcome page say "Sign in to open Balances", and the page, tab and all (`#places/prices` returns to Roll prices), survives the sign-in round trip when the address comes back bare (an address that names a page is never overridden, and a visit to the demo leaves the saved page waiting for the real ledger, as it does a bet link). When Google sign-in is switched off, the demo button becomes the primary button because it is the one thing that works. The welcome subtitle is balanced so no word is left alone on its second line, the Google button carries the four-colour mark on a white disc (hidden from assistive technology, so the button is named exactly "Continue with Google"), and at 360px and below the page tightens so both buttons fit a 640px-tall phone. The opening screen is a status region inside a main landmark in the page's ink at 14px; after eight seconds it says "Still opening the ledger…" and offers Try again, which hands focus to the status words as its button goes away and starts the count over.

In-app notices: a storage or connection problem appears as one notice with its next step as buttons, the same component the welcome page uses. A storage warning clears itself once a change has been saved properly (the standing "cannot coordinate demo tabs" notice returns after a save until it is dismissed), and carries Dismiss; the one that tells the member to export also carries Export, which opens the export dialog from any page. "Updates are paused" carries Reconnect, which asks the ledger for a fresh copy. A session that ends while the ledger is open (signed out in another tab, or it ran out) is a different notice, because Reconnect cannot help once the session is gone: a 401 on a refresh, or on a change, shows "Your session ended. Sign in again to keep the ledger up to date." with a Sign in again link, replaces the paused-updates line, keeps the records on screen, and brings the member back to the page they were on. When a notice's button goes away with the notice, focus moves to the page heading. If the demo's saved records cannot be read, the notice says the sample ledger replaced them, since that is what happened.

Bet links: `?bet=` takes a bet's id or its LL-#### reference. The parameter leaves the address bar as soon as the link is saved for the sign-in round trip (and again when the bet opens, which leaves the address on `#ledger`), so reloading never reopens the sheet. A signed-out visitor holding a link sees "Sign in to open the bet you were sent" above the Google button, as plain text with an icon (no border or fill, so it cannot be taken for a button). The demo neither saves a link nor takes or consumes the one saved, so one waiting for the real ledger survives a visit to the demo; it reads only a `?bet=` in its own address (the demo's own "Linked bets" and "Offset history" references keep `?demo=1`, so a new tab lands on the same bet in the same demo), opens that sheet, and takes the parameter out of the address bar the same way. A link whose value names nothing (a cut-off copy, or an empty `?bet=`) is reported, not dropped silently: a signed-out visitor sees a dismissible notice on the welcome page (Dismiss hands focus to the page heading), and a signed-in member an error toast, without leaving the page they were on. A link that names a bet the member cannot see is an error toast, which stays at least six seconds and can be dismissed. An open bet sheet is its own browser history entry: Back closes the sheet and stays on the page, which is the only way out on many phones. When the sheet holds a typed draft, Back asks "Discard what you have entered?" first, as Close does. If the sheet was left by a typed #hash, its entry stays behind in the history, and the next Back steps past it instead of spending a press on a page that shows nothing new. An address that was asked about and turned down leaves nothing behind: the sheet's entry takes the place of the entry the address made, so Back never lands on a page the member declined to visit, and Discard, which does go on to that address, opens it in that entry's place.

## Alternatives considered

Google Apps Script makes an internal spreadsheet-backed tool quick, but adds constraints to a custom application and transactional ledger. A static local prototype is useful for review but cannot establish real approvals. Cloudflare provides a portable TypeScript application, a transactional database, and a direct path to a shared private deployment.

## Implementation and verification

1. Establish a typed state machine and meaningful failing tests for approvals, judge permissions, prompt revisions, direct nontransferable debts, and physical settlement.

## Manual offsets

`POST /api/debt-offsets` accepts the preview's counterparty, equal amount per direction,
and per-bet allocations with expected versions. The server recomputes the maximum
eligible offset. An immutable `debt_offsets` record and all wager/audit updates are
committed in one SQL statement by migration `0007_manual_debt_offsets.sql`. A stale
version or audit insertion failure aborts the whole statement. Repeating an old
request conflicts rather than offsetting again. The SQL obligations view, application
balances, CSV export and delivery totals account for partial offsets. Existing
records without offsets retain their prior behavior. CLI and MCP reads expose
remaining debts; apply offsets through the website's Balances page. A pair that has debts both ways but a delivery still under confirmation shows a "Not available while a delivery on one of these bets is waiting for confirmation" row on the card instead of vanishing (a confirmed delivery settles that debt, so the wording does not promise the offset will come back), and adds "It is yours to confirm" when the confirmation is the viewer's.
